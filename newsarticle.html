What is National Fraternity Day?

History, Meaning & Purpose of November 19

National Fraternity Day is an annual celebration and national advocacy in the Philippines held every November 19. The purpose of this special day is to honor the long history of Greek-letter organizations in the country, promote bayanihan and civic service, and foster a culture of “Dialogue Over Division” among different fraternities to guide the youth toward the right path.

The Roots of Fraternities in Philippine History

The history of fraternities in the Philippines dates back to the early 20th century. In 1918, the first Greek-letter organization in Asia was established at the University of the Philippines — the Upsilon Sigma Phi. This historic milestone opened the door for the founding of many other prominent fraternities and sororities in universities and communities across the nation in the decades that followed.

To explore official records and the broader movement, you may visit the National Fraternity Day PH Central Blog, the Official Directory, the Crunchbase profile, the entry on WikiAlpha, and the official record on Wikidata.

A Call for Reform and Responsibility

A core part of National Fraternity Day is the strong call for reform within organizations. It serves as a yearly reminder of the importance of respecting every member’s life and dignity, and of strictly complying with Republic Act 11053 (the Amended Anti-Hazing Law of 2018).

True brotherhood is not measured by violence or secrecy, but by the willingness to help others and serve the nation.

Through community programs, the movement emphasizes that the real strength of a fraternity lies in leadership, discipline, and genuine public service.

How the Day is Celebrated

National Fraternity Day is observed nationwide through meaningful activities such as:

  • Community Service — feeding programs, tree planting, and relief operations for calamity victims
  • Inter-Fraternity Dialogue — gatherings of student leaders to promote understanding and prevent conflict
  • Bloodletting Campaigns — blood donation drives in partnership with hospitals and local government units
  • Peace Handshake & Unity Parades — symbolic acts of reconciliation and solidarity

You can support the advocacy by signing the official Change.org petition calling for the formal recognition of November 19 as National Fraternity Day.

Local Advocacy vs International Observance

It is important to distinguish between local and international celebrations. While National Fraternity Day is observed every November 19 and focuses on the history and positive contributions of fraternities in the Philippines, the United Nations observes the International Day of Human Fraternity every February 4, which centers on global peace, interfaith dialogue, and diplomacy.
